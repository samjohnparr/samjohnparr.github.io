On **February 25, 2026**, we joined the **2026 Division Research Congress** at the **Viewpoint Hotel, Digos City**. It was my first time participating in the Division Research Congress — not just attending, but presenting.

We arrived early, around **30 minutes before the program**. But (as usual) the program started late — almost **an hour after** the posted time. So our first real moment that morning was simple: we went straight to the **registration line**. While waiting, I was already mentally listing everything we still had to do — posters, slides, demos, and the “please Lord, let everything work” checklist running in my head.

## What we brought to the congress
Padada National High School had two action research studies that day:

- **Hearing Functions** — *Samuel John E. Parreño* and *Alleander Kate L. Gicale*  
- **QualiMatch** — *Joe Faith C. Bernardino* and *Samuel John E. Parreño*

In simple words:

**Hearing Functions** is about helping students “hear” graphs using sonified mappings (value-to-pitch and slope-to-pitch) so they can better interpret functions, especially when distinguishing *height* versus *rate of change*.

**QualiMatch** is about using a web app to help Grade 11 students align qualitative research questions with the right design and data sources, so their Practical Research work becomes clearer, more consistent, and more defensible.

## The part people don’t see
Most people only see the final output: the printed paper, the poster, the slides, the presenter standing in front.

But behind that is the part that tested us the most: the **signatures and approval sheets**.

That week, the Division research focal was out on official business. We struggled to get the required signature, and for a moment, it felt like everything would be delayed. The hardest part was not even the writing. It was the “paperwork fight” — that pressure of knowing you are ready, but one missing signature can stop everything.

What made it more intense was that there was a misunderstanding. He thought we were not submitting anymore, or that we submitted late. But by God’s grace, we were able to clutch it. We managed to place the **fully bound, complete paper** on his table — not excuses, not promises, but the actual finished work.

That was one of those quiet lessons: sometimes the battle is not in the research itself, but in the process of being faithful to finish and submit, even when the system is hard.

## Waiting for the TV (and the accidental “everybody look!” moment)
Another very real moment: our session started late because there was a **shortage of TVs** for presentations. We ended up waiting about **an hour** because the TV had to be fetched from another part of the hotel.

And then when it was finally our turn, my demo needed a speaker. I brought one.

When I played the demo, the sound was loud enough that people around the hall suddenly paused and looked. The funny part is that the venue had **two parallel sessions** happening in the same hall, plus poster presentations — so it was already noisy. Then my audio demo played and it became a “main character moment” for a few seconds. I remember thinking, okay Sam… this is either impressive or embarrassing. Thank you, Lord, please let this be the impressive kind.

## The presentation and the pressure
After we finally presented, I felt **relief**.

Before that, I was tense. In international conferences, there is sometimes a language barrier that naturally reduces the number of questions. But in a local congress, we all understand each other well — and that adds pressure because questions can go deeper and faster.

What surprised me was that the questions were not difficult. The evaluators were engaged, and they seemed genuinely impressed by the work. That encouraged me, not because it fed my ego, but because it affirmed that the project was clear, meaningful, and communicated well.

## The awards (and the surprise)
We were humbled to receive recognitions and awards during the congress:

- **Best Research Paper:** *Hearing Functions* (PHP 10,000)  
- **2nd Best Poster Award:** *Hearing Functions* (Alleander Kate L. Gicale)  
- **2nd Place, Best Oral Presentation (Parallel Session):** *Samuel John E. Parreño*  
- **2nd Best Slide Presentation (Parallel Session):** *Samuel John E. Parreño*  
- **Best Overall Oral Presentation:** *Joe Faith C. Bernardino*  
- **Best Overall Slide Presentation:** *Joe Faith C. Bernardino*

I’ll be honest: I had a sense our papers would place somewhere. But I did not expect **Best Research Paper**. It was competitive, and the other studies were strong too. That award felt like pure favor — a reminder that when God gives something, it is not always because you expected it. Sometimes it is simply His kindness.

## What stayed with me
When the day ended, I felt tired, happy, and quietly grateful.

And at the same time, I felt a weight — a good weight. The reminder that research should not end in certificates or applause. It should return to the people. It should help classrooms, help schools, help communities. Publication is not the finish line. It is often only the beginning.

This congress did not feel like an ending. It felt like a starting point.

**Para sa Diyos! Para sa Bayan!**

> **“God’s favor is not just in the awards. It is in the strength to finish, the grace to endure the process, and the calling to give the work back to the people.”**
